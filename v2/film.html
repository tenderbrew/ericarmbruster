Galaxy Quest (1999)
★★★★ · Jun 11, 2026

Not just a great Star Trek parody, also a fine film in its own right. Extremely funny bits that still cause belly laughs over 25 years later and definitively better than any actual Star Trek property since its release. Just a ton of whimsy and supremely rewatchable. The special effects are highly commendable splitting the line between the TV set aesthetic and the real world superbly. A comedy classic.

The Fly (1986)
★★★★★ · Jun 10, 2026

A Cronenberg classic. Jeff Goldblum before he fully became "Jeff Goldblum". The chemistry with Geena Davis is delightful. The special effects are magnificent and disgusting, completely on-brand. Many unique and quintessential scenes such as the arm wrestling scene and the final showdown after snatching Veronica from the hospital and of course the teleportation sequences. This is a rewatch for me, but a rewatch far removed from the last time I saw it, probably over 20 years ago. Still completely works and it probably won't be another 20 before I watch it again.

Masters of the Universe (2026)
★★ · Jun 9, 2026

Convinced to go on $5 tickets to a 4DX showing (first one I've been to.) 4DX was pretty rad, this film was all over the place though. First off, 2.5 hours? Why? What are we doing here? This legit gets at least 1 more star from being 90 minutes. It wasn't all bad, but we've really suffered under the effects of millennial/Joss Whedon writing for far too long. Maybe people in the 50s thought this about 50s film by the time it was all over, who knows? Anyway it's competently entertaining, but far from good or well-executed. It wasn't as awful as the trailers made it out to be and that's the kindest thing I can say about it.

Nirvanna the Band the Show the Movie (2025)
★★★ · Jun 8, 2026

A fun little comedy adventure completely layered with good bits. This feels like a film begging to be watched a few times to really catch all of them. The really surprising part though is the insanely great looking special effects. Floored by how this film looks. Some day hopefully I come across a behind-the-scenes that goes into more detail, but its crazy to say - this looks better than many modern big budget films. I've only ever really seen one bit from the web show (well represented here in a scene, the 'Wii Shop Wednesday' song), so maybe I didn't fully absorb the total context the first time through, but worthy of my time and will probably revisit soon.

The Social Network (2010)
★★★★ · Jun 7, 2026

Was too hard on this one when it first came out. It was like seeing a 9/11 film in 2003. The real events were too recent and in this case, I was also there in a sense because I started using Facebook as our school was one of the early adopters. Now, in the year 2026 it feels much more like a retrospective and I was able to more subtly enjoy David Fincher's direction and storytelling. Jumping between two legal proceedings and intertwining that with the story works really well and keeps a tension throughout. The score fit the awkward atmosphere with some neat understated industrial and synth tones.

Marty (1955)
★★★ · Jun 6, 2026

People in the 50s found this guy comically obese. People in the 50s found this woman horrifically ugly. Very odd, but uplifting flick. Basically feels like a more pathetic Rocky before Rocky (and no boxing). Dude's friends were all losers and his family ended up being losers as well. This apparently won Best Picture, too. I found it, at times very funny and at times very heartfelt, but I have to admit, it never completely had me. Low stakes flick for a quiet Sunday.

Paths of Glory (1957)
★★★★★ · Jun 5, 2026

Set up to expect a movie about soldiers taking a position against all odds. Remarkably betrayed. In the end, this single event never mattered. The only thing that mattered was the consequences of inevitably failing. A brilliant exploration of early modern warfare. Distinct contrast between commanders and soldiers. Set up at every moment to perplex you. A hopeless tale where there is obviously no good ending - they make sure to accentuate that from as early as possible, but as human beings, we're tricked to believe maybe some twist or reckoning occurs. Not so. Stunning cinematography and wonderfully directed. Ending punctuated with disarming humanity. Truly a beautiful film, with some amazing performances. Powerful in its anti-war position, but not ham fisted in its execution. A true surprise.

Grave of the Fireflies (1988)
★★★★ · May 30, 2026

Finally getting around to this one. As a big fan of other Ghibli films, was very interested about how this would play out, knowing its reputation. It definitely has that core Ghibli feel to it and is a 90-minute cataclysmic descent as we watch a struggling 14-year-old boy and his young sister try and survive on their own during war time in Japan. There's definitely a nagging sense of confusion in the film as we watch Seita doing everything he can for his sister, while simultaneously also making seemingly baffling decisions at every turn. Ultimately, it ends in a slow grinding tragic end. That's not to say there isn't intense beauty in this film - there certainly is and that contrast just makes the narrative that much more memorable: the titular scene inside the cave, the montage of Setsuko at the bomb shelter near the conclusion, flashbacks to moments with their parents. A remarkable film about losing not only family and community, but all hope.
